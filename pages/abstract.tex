\begin{abstract} 
    Virtualisation is a technology developed to share the
    capabilities of a physical computer by dividing its
    resources among multiple operating systems. This paper
    aims to provide an overview of a few virtualisation
    strategies from the perspective of the operating system,
    tracing the concept from IBM’s project in the 1960 to the
    principles established by Popek and Goldberg in their
    papers. This paper examines distinct execution methods
    including the use of modified guest OS’s through the
    para-virtualization and hypercalls as well the execution of
    unmodified guest OS’s through binary translation and
    hardware assisted virtualisation. Furthermore, this paper
    will showcase the classification of Virtual Machines
    Monitors abbreviated as VMMs into type I and type II
    hypervisors based on their interactions with the hardware
    level and the host operating system. In the end, the paper
    talks a little about the technical challenges of classical
    virtualization specifically focusing on de-privileging,
    shadow structures and the complex overhead involved in
    the translation of x86 memory addresses and paging
    systems.
    \end{abstract}
