\begin{abstract}
Virtualisation is a technology developed to share the capabilities of a physical computer by dividing its resources among multiple operating systems. This paper aims to provide an overview of a few virtualisation strategies from the perspective of the operating system, tracing the concept from IBM’s project in the 1960 to the principles established by Popek and Goldberg in their papers. 


This study presents an academic and technical analysis of how computer system virtualisation works covering topics such as their historical evolution, hardware protection rings, virtual network design and security. On top of those topics such as modern virtualisation methods focusing on shadow pages, nested paging, agile paging and remote paging, I/O operations with a focus on I/O Passthrough and IOMMU hardware assisted support via Intel VT-d and AMD-Vi.
\end{abstract}
